## 3. The Cauchy Distributional Continuum Limit

### 3.1 Nuclear Montel Fréchet Space $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$

The space of tempered distributions $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$ is the topological strong dual of the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing smooth functions $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$. 1. **Completeness**: Every Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$ converges to a unique limit distribution.
2. **Heine–Borel Property**: Every closed and bounded subset is compact.
3. **Distributional Dual Norm**: The topology is generated by a family of seminorms indexed by bounded sets $B \subset \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$:

$$
\|T\|_{B} = \sup_{f \in B} |T(f)|.

### 3.2 The Dyadic Cauchy Sequence Bound

Let $S_n^{(a_k)} \in \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$ denote the $n$-point Schwinger distribution evaluated on the lattice with spacing $a_k = 2^{-k} a_0$. $$
\left| S_n^{(a_{k+1})}(f) - S_n^{(a_k)}(f) \right| \le C_n \, \|f\|_{\mathcal{S}, N} \cdot 2^{-k},

where $C_n < \infty$ is a constant independent of $k$, and $\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}, N}$ is a standard Schwartz seminorm. Summing the geometric series between arbitrary dyadic indices $j \le k$:

$$

### 3.3 Constructive Uniqueness of the Limit Distribution

Because $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$ is topologically complete, the Cauchy sequence $\{S_n^{(a_k)}\}_{k=0}^\infty$ converges to a **unique** limit distribution $S_n \in \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{4n})$:

$$
