## 2. Theoretical Foundations of Discrete Exterior Calculus

### 2.1 Graded Cell Complexes and Combinatorial Chains

Let $K$ denote a finite cell complex of topological dimension $d$. The space of combinatorial $k$-chains, denoted $C_k(K, \mathbb{R})$, is the finite-dimensional real vector space generated by the oriented $k$-cells $\sigma_i^k \in K$:

$$
C_k(K, \mathbb{R}) = \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^{N_k} c_i \, \sigma_i^k \;\middle|\; c_i \in \mathbb{R} \right\}, \quad N_k = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} C_k(K, \mathbb{R}).
$$

Orientation is encoded by assigning a skew-symmetric sign under permutations of vertex orderings. The combinatorial boundary operator $\partial_k : C_k(K, \mathbb{R}) \to C_{k-1}(K, \mathbb{R})$ acts linearly on basis cells $\sigma^k = [v_0, v_1, \dots, v_k]$ according to the classical simplicial formula:

$$
\partial_k [v_0, v_1, \dots, v_k] = \sum_{j=0}^k (-1)^j [v_0, \dots, \hat{v}_j, \dots, v_k],
$$

where $\hat{v}_j$ indicates the omission of the $j$-th vertex. The fundamental topological identity of the complex is the nilpotency of the boundary operator:

$$
\partial_{k-1} \circ \partial_k = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1.
$$

In the ToposLang engine, this boundary property is verified unconditionally over exact integer arithmetic, ensuring that all subsequent homology groups $H_k(K) = \ker \partial_k / \mathrm{im} \, \partial_{k+1}$ are strictly well-defined.

### 2.2 Cochains, Coboundary, and the Discrete de Rham Complex

The dual vector space $C^k(K, \mathbb{R}) = \mathrm{Hom}(C_k(K, \mathbb{R}), \mathbb{R})$ constitutes the space of discrete $k$-cochains, which serve as the discrete analogues of differential $k$-forms. The combinatorial coboundary operator $d^k : C^k(K, \mathbb{R}) \to C^{k+1}(K, \mathbb{R})$ is defined via the algebraic adjoint of the boundary operator under the natural pairing $\langle \omega, c \rangle = \omega(c)$:

$$
\langle d^k \omega, c \rangle = \langle \omega, \partial_{k+1} c \rangle \quad \forall \, \omega \in C^k(K, \mathbb{R}), \; c \in C_{k+1}(K, \mathbb{R}).
$$

Consequently, the coboundary operator inherits nilpotency from $\partial$:

$$
d^{k+1} \circ d^k = (\partial_{k+1} \circ \partial_{k+2})^* = 0.
$$

This nilpotency guarantees that the sequence of cochain spaces forms a cochain complex $(C^\bullet(K), d^\bullet)$, whose cohomology groups $H^k(K, \mathbb{R})$ are isomorphic to the singular cohomology of the underlying topological space by the discrete de Rham theorem.

## 3. Discrete Hodge Theory and the Hodge Decomposition Theorem

### 3.1 Inner Products and the Discrete Hodge Star Operator

To define metric geometry, Laplacians, and harmonic forms on a combinatorial complex, an inner product must be endowed on the chain and cochain spaces. Given a geometric embedding of $K$ with dual Voronoi complex $\star K$, the discrete Hodge star operator is an isomorphism:

$$
\star_k : C^k(K, \mathbb{R}) \to C^{d-k}(\star K, \mathbb{R}),
$$

satisfying the geometric normalization condition:

$$
\star_k \sigma^k = \frac{|\star \sigma^k|}{|\sigma^k|} \, (\star \sigma^k),
$$

where $|\sigma^k|$ denotes the Euclidean volume of the primal $k$-cell, and $|\star \sigma^k|$ denotes the volume of the dual $(d-k)$-cell. The induced inner product on $C^k(K, \mathbb{R})$ is defined by:

$$
\langle \alpha, \beta \rangle_{C^k} = \sum_{\sigma^k \in K} |\sigma^k| \, \alpha(\sigma^k) \, (\star_k \beta)(\star \sigma^k).
$$

On uniform or abstract simplicial complexes with standard orthonormal bases, the discrete inner product reduces to the Euclidean $\ell^2$ dot product on cochain coefficient vectors.

### 3.2 The Discrete Codifferential and Hodge Laplacian

The discrete codifferential operator $\delta_k : C^k(K, \mathbb{R}) \to C^{k-1}(K, \mathbb{R})$ is the formal adjoint of the coboundary operator $d^{k-1}$ with respect to the cochain inner product:

$$
\langle d^{k-1} \alpha, \beta \rangle_{C^k} = \langle \alpha, \delta_k \beta \rangle_{C^{k-1}} \quad \forall \, \alpha \in C^{k-1}(K), \; \beta \in C^k(K).
$$

In matrix representation with respect to orthonormal cochain bases, the codifferential is given simply by the matrix transpose:

$$
\delta_k = (d^{k-1})^\top.
$$

The discrete Hodge Laplacian operator $\Delta_k : C^k(K, \mathbb{R}) \to C^k(K, \mathbb{R})$ is defined by the discrete Laplace–de Rham formula:

$$
\Delta_k = d^{k-1} \delta_k + \delta_{k+1} d^k = d^{k-1} (d^{k-1})^\top + (d^k)^\top d^k.
$$

The discrete Hodge decomposition theorem states that the cochain space decomposes into a mutually orthogonal direct sum of exact, harmonic, and coexact subspaces:

$$
C^k(K, \mathbb{R}) = \mathrm{im}(d^{k-1}) \oplus \ker(\Delta_k) \oplus \mathrm{im}(\delta_{k+1}).
$$

Furthermore, every cohomology class $[\omega] \in H^k(K, \mathbb{R})$ contains a unique harmonic representative $\omega_h \in \ker(\Delta_k)$.
